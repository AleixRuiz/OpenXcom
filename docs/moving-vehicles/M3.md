# Moving Vehicles — M3: Turning and flying

Last updated 2026-10-30 · Aleix Ruiz Sorribes

M3 is the milestone that lets vehicles turn, and later fly ([design](DESIGN.md#roadmap)). Turning comes first, so the convoy showcase can show a Chimera driving the way it faces, all the way round. Like M2, it is built before the pitch ([upstream strategy](DESIGN.md#upstream-strategy)), on top of the core and M2, and leaves vehicles without the new keys exactly as they were.

## What the player can do

- Turn a vehicle in drive mode with a right click toward where it should face, as a unit turns. A south-facing Chimera turns east by mirroring its map, with no new art.
- With per-facing maps from the mod, turn it all the way round: the showcase Chimera faces north and west too, showing its back.
- Turn where there's room: turning is a step, so the turned vehicle has to fit, crushing, running over or being blocked like any step.
- Drive the way it faces: a drive turns the vehicle toward where it goes first (proposed, Phase 17).
- Fly, later in M3.

## Facings and art

The battle camera looks from the south-east, so a map shows the south and east faces of what is on it. Rosigma's Chimera exists drawn facing south (the player's Chimera) and facing east (the traitor convoy's Chimeras): front and both flanks are drawn, its back never is.

- **Mirroring** swaps the two faces seen from the same side: south and east, or north and west. It can't turn a vehicle round, because its back was never drawn.
- **Four drawn maps** are the most a vehicle can have, and the showcase Chimera gets them all.
- **Fewer maps:** a missing facing is mirrored from its partner. A vehicle drawn only facing the camera (south or east, the usual case) turns between those two, and drives in reverse when it goes north or west.

## Art for the showcase

The north and west Chimera are drafted by an AI image tool, and our scripts do everything around it:

1. **References and templates (scripts).** We render the existing Chimera facing south and east, at the game's scale, and a grey template of it facing north and west. The template is built from the tiles' hit shapes turned round, so its outline and proportions match the tile grid exactly.
2. **Painting (AI tool, by the user).** The recommended tool is Google's Nano Banana Pro (Gemini 3 Pro Image). It is good at showing a referenced object from a new viewpoint, and takes several reference images. Give it the template and the two references, upscaled 4 times so the pixels stay crisp, and ask for the template painted as the back of the same vehicle, in the same style and colours. Fallback when its drafts drift from the template: an image-to-3D tool that takes several views (Tripo 3.1 Multi View or Meshy 6), rendered at the game's angle as a new template.
3. **Into the game (scripts).** We bring the painted views into the game's palette and cut them into tiles along the template, give each tile the hit shape of the tile it turns into, and write the tilesets and maps for the showcase mod.

Done on 2026-10-30 with Nano Banana Pro through Google's API: five images, about $0.70. North took a first draft and one edit (a roof emblem removed, the round hatch moved above the rear door); west was right first time. The scripts are in [tools/art](tools/art/README.md); the drafts and the tiles cut from them stay in the local showcase mod.

Tools made for pixel-art sprites, such as PixelLab's rotation, were looked at and left aside: they work on images up to about 128 to 168 pixels, and a whole Chimera is about twice that.

The drafts are Rosigma's art, so they stay in the showcase mod; using them in Rosigma is the Rosigma team's call.

## Decisions taken for M3

- **Turning is a step.** It fits or it doesn't, pays `tuPerQuarterTurn` per quarter turn, crushes and runs over like a step, and draws reaction fire like one ([GD-30](GAME_DESIGN_DECISIONS.md#gd-30--turning-is-a-step-and-mirroring-is-free-art)).
- **Turning is opt-in.** Only a vehicle with `front` turns; the others stay exactly as in M1 and M2.
- **Up to four drawn facings, mirroring fills the gaps, reverse when there's no back** ([GD-32](GAME_DESIGN_DECISIONS.md#gd-32--up-to-four-drawn-facings-mirroring-fills-the-gaps), the user's call).
- **Mirror turning is the default; drawn facings are optional and can be dropped.** They only apply with `facingMaps`, and Phase 18 lives in its own commit (GD-32).
- **Whether a drive turns the vehicle by itself is settled after Phase 16 has been played** ([GD-31](GAME_DESIGN_DECISIONS.md#gd-31--drives-turn-the-vehicle-toward-where-it-goes) stays proposed until Phase 17).

## Demo mods

- **Rosigma:** the Chimeras and the Baneblade get `front: 4` (they face south), the traitor convoy `front: 2` (east). Its own file, `facings.rul`, gives the showcase Chimera its north and west maps (Phase 18); their tilesets are Rosigma's art and stay out of the repository, so without them the Chimera turns by mirroring. Delete `facings.rul` to go back to mirror turning.
- **Vanilla:** the Skyranger gets `front: 4`, to show mirror turning without Rosigma.

## Done when (turning)

- [ ] A vehicle with `front` turns with a right click in drive mode, between the facings its art shows, paying `tuPerQuarterTurn` per quarter turn; one without `front` never turns.
- [ ] A turned vehicle looks right, hits right (shots, walls, doors) and carries its units and items, which turn with it.
- [ ] A turn that doesn't fit is blocked; one onto enemies runs them over, as a step does.
- [ ] Battles saved with turned vehicles load with every vehicle as it was.
- [ ] The showcase Chimera turns all the way round with its four drawn facings.
- [ ] Every new ruleset key is described in the design document.
- [ ] The Linux build (GCC with OXCE's warnings) has no warnings in our code.
